In Memory of Frank Delaney

From 2010 to 2016, Frank Delaney created Re:Joyce, an extraordinary podcast series that walked readers through James Joyce's Ulysses sentence by sentence, illuminating its multilayered meanings with warmth, scholarship, and infectious enthusiasm.

Delaney's death in 2017 left the project unfinished, ending at Episode 6, "Hades." This site continues in his spirit—drawing from scholarly sources to guide readers through all eighteen episodes of Joyce's modernist masterpiece.

The Episodes of Ulysses

Joyce structured Ulysses into eighteen episodes, each corresponding to an episode in Homer's Odyssey. The novel takes place over a single day—June 16, 1904—in Dublin, following Leopold Bloom, Stephen Dedalus, and Molly Bloom through their ordinary yet profound experiences.

About This Project

In Memory of Frank Delaney

Frank Delaney (1942-2017) was an Irish writer and broadcaster whose podcast Re:Joyce became an invaluable companion for readers tackling Joyce's notoriously challenging masterwork. With patience, erudition, and genuine love for the material, Delaney guided listeners through the opening episodes of Ulysses, explaining historical references, unpacking literary allusions, and revealing the extraordinary artistry in Joyce's prose.

His death in 2017 left the project incomplete, but his approach—careful, generous, and deeply appreciative of both Joyce's genius and the reader's effort—continues to inspire.

About Ulysses

Published in 1922, James Joyce's Ulysses chronicles a single day—June 16, 1904—in the life of Leopold Bloom, a Jewish advertising canvasser in Dublin. Paralleling Homer's Odyssey, the novel transforms an ordinary day into an epic of consciousness, experimenting with narrative technique, language, and form in ways that revolutionized modern literature.

Each of its eighteen episodes employs a different literary style or technique, ranging from stream of consciousness to catechism, from parodies of English prose styles to musical fugue. The result is challenging, rewarding, and endlessly rich.

Sources & Acknowledgments

This site draws from several invaluable scholarly resources:

  • The Joyce Project - A comprehensive, freely available online resource curated by Professor John Hunt of the University of Montana. The Joyce Project provides the full text of Ulysses with scholarly annotations, images, character lists, and detailed episode introductions. Many annotations and contextual insights on this site are drawn from or inspired by The Joyce Project's excellent scholarship.
  • Don Gifford's Ulysses Annotated - The standard reference work for Joyce scholars, providing encyclopedic annotations covering literary allusions, historical references, Dublin topography, slang, and much more
  • The Linati and Gilbert Schemas - Joyce's own frameworks for understanding the novel's structure, showing the Homeric parallels, organs, arts, colors, symbols, and techniques for each episode
  • Historical and geographical references about 1904 Dublin, Irish politics, and cultural context
  • Contemporary Joyce scholarship from various academic sources

All content is presented for educational purposes, with proper attribution to sources. The text of Ulysses (published 1922) is in the public domain.

Special thanks to The Joyce Project for making high-quality Joyce scholarship freely available to readers worldwide.
